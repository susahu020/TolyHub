SIP Calculator — Estimate Your Mutual Fund Returns

See how a small monthly investment can grow over time — based on your SIP amount, expected return rate, and investment period.

A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) lets you invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals — usually monthly — instead of investing a lump sum all at once. Because each instalment buys units at a different price, SIPs average out market ups and downs over time, while the power of compounding lets even small, consistent investments grow substantially over the long run.

Our SIP Calculator projects the future value of your investment based on the monthly amount, expected annual return rate, and investment period you enter, using the standard SIP compounding formula. It also supports step-up SIPs, where you increase your monthly instalment by a fixed percentage every year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula is used to calculate SIP returns?

The standard SIP future value formula is M = P × [(((1+i)^n − 1) / i)] × (1+i), where P is the monthly investment, i is the monthly rate of return (annual rate divided by 12, then by 100), and n is the total number of months invested.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up (or top-up) SIP is one where you increase your monthly investment amount by a fixed percentage every year, typically in line with rising income, instead of keeping it constant for the entire tenure. This can significantly increase the final maturity value compared to a regular SIP.

Are the returns from this calculator guaranteed?

No. This calculator projects a future value based on the expected annual return rate you enter, assuming that rate stays constant. Actual mutual fund returns fluctuate with market performance and are never guaranteed — use this tool for planning and estimation only.

Does this calculator account for expense ratio, exit load, or taxes?

No, this calculator estimates gross returns based on your investment amount, rate, and tenure only. Actual take-home returns will be lower once fund expense ratio, applicable exit load, and capital gains tax are factored in — check with your fund house or advisor for exact post-cost figures.
