The 60-Day Booking Rule, in Plain Terms

Indian Railways currently allows booking up to 60 days before the journey date, not counting the journey date itself. This was reduced from the older 120-day window in late 2024, specifically to cut down on speculative bookings that were never actually used. So if you're travelling on a date 61 days from today, you can book today. If you're travelling 59 days from today, booking already opened yesterday.

The detail people most often get wrong: the count excludes the travel date and runs from the train's originating station, not necessarily your own boarding station. For most journeys that doesn't matter, but on a long-distance train where you board a day or two after it leaves its origin, your effective booking-open date can land a day earlier than a simple "60 days from when I board" calculation suggests.

Tatkal Timing — Why Minutes Matter

Tatkal is a separate emergency quota, not an extension of the normal booking window. It opens exactly one day before departure, and the opening time depends on class:

  • AC classes (1A, 2A, 3A, Chair Car, Executive Chair Car) — opens at 10:00 AM IST.
  • Non-AC classes (Sleeper, Second Sitting) — opens at 11:00 AM IST.

The staggered timing exists so the booking system isn't hit by every passenger at once. On popular routes, seats in the Tatkal quota can disappear within the first few minutes, so being online and authenticated before the window opens matters more here than almost anywhere else in the booking process. Aadhaar-based OTP authentication is now required for online Tatkal bookings, and authorised agents are blocked from booking during the first 30 minutes of each window — both changes aimed at giving genuine passengers a fairer shot.

What You Actually Get Back If You Cancel

Cancellation refunds work on a sliding scale — the earlier you cancel, the more you keep. Under the rules in effect from April 2026, for a confirmed (non-Tatkal) ticket:

  • More than 72 hours before departure — only a small flat cancellation charge is deducted (varies by class); you keep the rest.
  • 72 to 24 hours before departure — 25% of the fare is deducted.
  • 24 to 8 hours before departure — 50% of the fare is deducted.
  • Less than 8 hours before departure — no refund.

Confirmed Tatkal tickets sit outside this entire scale: they're non-refundable on voluntary cancellation no matter how early you cancel. That's the trade-off for booking in the emergency quota in the first place. RAC and waitlisted tickets — Tatkal or otherwise — follow smaller, separate deductions, since the seat was never guaranteed to begin with.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days in advance can I book an IRCTC train ticket?

Most trains follow the current 60-day Advance Reservation Period (ARP), counted backward from the journey date and excluding the journey date itself. Booking opens at 8:00 AM IST on the opening day. A small number of special or premium trains may follow a different ARP set separately by Indian Railways.

What time does Tatkal booking open?

Tatkal booking opens one day before the journey date (counted from the train's originating station). AC classes (1A, 2A, 3A, CC, EC) open at 10:00 AM IST, and non-AC classes (Sleeper, Second Sitting) open at 11:00 AM IST.

Do I get a refund if I cancel a Tatkal ticket?

No. Confirmed Tatkal tickets are non-refundable on voluntary cancellation, regardless of how far in advance you cancel. RAC or waitlisted Tatkal tickets are refunded after a small clerkage deduction, since they were never actually confirmed.

How much refund do I get if I cancel a confirmed ticket?

Under the cancellation rules effective April 2026, cancelling more than 72 hours before departure incurs only a flat charge (full refund minus a small fee). Cancelling 72–24 hours before departure deducts 25% of the fare. Cancelling 24–8 hours before deducts 50%. Cancelling less than 8 hours before departure gives no refund.

Is this calculator affiliated with IRCTC or Indian Railways?

No. This is an independent, unofficial calculator built for convenience based on publicly available Indian Railways policy. Booking rules can change, and some trains have exceptions, so always confirm the exact dates and amounts on the official IRCTC website or app before relying on them for a real booking.
